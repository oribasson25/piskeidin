## Design Context

### Users
Legal assistants and clerks at Libra, a law firm. They process large batches of court verdict PDFs daily, extracting structured data (court, judge, case description, verdict, payment amount) for export to Excel. Daily, repeated use — efficiency and scannability matter more than novelty.

### Brand Personality
Libra (ליברה) — the scales of justice. Three words: **rigorous, institutional, systematic**. The tool is a professional legal instrument, not a consumer product. Emotional goal: confidence and authority.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light mode — law firm office, daytime use, document-intensive work
- **Ground**: Warm paper background (`oklch(97.2% 0.005 85)`) — connects to physical legal documents
- **Primary color**: Deep institutional navy (`oklch(26% 0.09 258)`) — authoritative, trustworthy
- **Display typeface**: Frank Ruhl Libre — the canonical Israeli serif used in legal publications, newspapers, and government documents. Immediately distinctive in a digital context.
- **Body typeface**: Rubik — clean, precise, works well in Hebrew and Latin
- **Radius**: Tight (3–8px), not rounded bubbles — institutional, not consumer
- **No decorative elements**: structure and typography carry the design

### Design Principles
1. **Legibility first** — the table is the product; every visual decision serves scannability
2. **Restrained authority** — color is used to signal status (navy=action, green=amount, red=error), not decoration
3. **Typographic hierarchy** — Frank Ruhl Libre for all headings and table headers creates an immediately distinctive, non-generic feel
4. **Information density** — this is a daily-use batch tool; leave room for data, not chrome
5. **Physical paper heritage** — warm ground and serif display font connect the digital tool to the physical legal document world
